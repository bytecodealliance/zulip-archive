pchickey opened issue #15140:

For historical reasons, Wasmtime's pooling allocator and the Engine struct are joined at the hip. Some aspects that affect pooling are indeed linked to codegen and therefore an Engine, but other aspects are not necessarily linked and, in fact this makes some kinds of configurations - the reasonable problem statement "I want a pooling allocator which can provide N instances of Component X" - unnecessarily difficult, or require crude hacks (e.g. serializing and deserializing a Component to move it between code-compatible Engines).

My rough design sketch:

  • Engine construction still locks in all aspects of a Module and Component's compilation that affect codegen. This is a very important design principle of Wasmtime we should consider basically set in stone.
  • There can be more than one allocator of Stores/Instances associated with an Engine, and the lifetime of those can be shorter than the Engine.
  • An Engine can be used for Component::new, and then a pooling allocator (as a new struct wasmtime::Pool) can be constructed based on Component::required_resources and the number of slots desired - basically, we separate out all aspects of PoolingAllocatorConfig that do not affect codegen, and make that construct a Pool, not an Engine. Notionally, Pool::new_store(&self, data) -> Result<Store<T>> works like Store::new(&Engine, data) -> Store<T>, failing when the pool does not have available slots.
  • In the absence of Pool use, all of the existing Engine machinery works with the on-demand allocator.
